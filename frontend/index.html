About and privacy

What FAHAM does

FAHAM reads a letter or message you give it, looks for signs of a scam, explains it in plain words in BM, English, Chinese or Tamil, and shows the usual steps to deal with it online or at an office. It can write a reminder and a draft reply for you to copy.

What it cannot do

  • It cannot see who really sent the message, open a link or call a number.
  • It cannot prove a letter is real. "No scam signs found" is not a promise.
  • It cannot send, pay or contact anyone for you. Nothing is sent automatically.
  • It can miss new tricks. When unsure, ask the agency yourself using a number or website you looked up.

How a verdict is decided

A fixed set of rules checks the text. An optional AI service may only reword the explanation. It does not decide the verdict.

Signs of a scam we look for:

  • Asks you to transfer money to a personal or company account
  • Shortened links or web addresses that are not .gov.my
  • Threats of arrest or "pay in 24 hours" pressure
  • Asks for your TAC or OTP, bank login, or a photo of your IC
  • Asks you to keep it secret or to use WhatsApp
  • A reference number that does not match the agency's format

Three possible answers: "Likely a scam" when strong signs are found. "Be careful" when we are not sure. "No scam signs found" when none of the above appears, which still does not prove the message is real.

Limits: the rules read words only. A well-written scam can pass, and a real letter can look odd after a photo is misread.

Your data

  • Personal details (IC, phone, account numbers) are masked before anything else happens.
  • Letters are not stored on our side. Your dictionary, points, language and tick are saved only in this browser.
  • Examples are fictional.

Guidance, not legal advice

Steps come from a cached copy of official sources, shown with the date and a confidence level. Check the official site before you act. Chinese and Tamil text still needs review by native speakers.
